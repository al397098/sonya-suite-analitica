Introduction to SONYA

Before opening the application, understand the logic behind it

SONYA is not an average calculator. It is a platform that starts from a very specific premise: when you evaluate the impact of an organisation, you cannot compensate for environmental damage with good economic results. The three pillars must perform well at the same time. That is the core of the model.

Imagine you have three candidates for a job. Candidate A scores 9 for experience and 8 for skills, but 1 for attitude. Most selection methods would hire them anyway because the average (6) is high. SONYA does not. For SONYA, that candidate does not pass: the weakest link defines the outcome.

1.1 The three perspectives of sustainability

Every alternative in SONYA is described with exactly three numbers. All three are "cost" criteria: the lower the value, the better the performance. There are no exceptions here.

Perspective What it Measures Example Unit Criterion
Environmental Negative impact on the environment: emissions, waste, pollution. kg CO₂ eq / unit produced Minimise
Social Negative social impact: workplace accidents, exclusion, conflict. Incidents / 1,000 workers Minimise
Economic Cost or economic burden of the alternative. € / tonne treated Minimise

⚠ WARNING: All three are cost criteria

The lower the value, the better the position in the ranking in all three cases. If you enter the values backwards (for example, a very high Social thinking it is positive), the engines will penalise it.

1.2 Why you cannot compensate one pillar with another

The idea of compensation is intuitive: if my company is very good environmentally, it can "pay for" mediocre social performance. This approach makes sense in some contexts, but in organisational sustainability it is problematic: a company with terrible social impact remains unsustainable, even if it has the world's best solar panels.

SONYA implements this logic through two mathematical mechanisms: the Leontief Operator (MRP Engine) and the Chebyshev distance (TOPSIS Engine). Both have the same essential property: the criterion with the worst performance determines the final result.

Database Management Module

Where your data lives before being analysed

3.1 Action vs. Functional Unit: they are not the same

Let us see the difference with an example:

ACTION FUNCTIONAL UNIT
The name of the project or scenario you wish to evaluate. The reference magnitude against which impacts are compared.
"Municipal Waste Management 2025" "kg CO₂ equivalent per tonne of waste treated"
"Sustainable Urban Mobility Plan" "grams of NOx emitted per passenger-kilometre"
It is the logical container: it groups all its alternatives. It defines the quantitative framework: allows comparing different alternatives in the same units.
Mandatory. Without it there is nothing to analyse. Optional. But without it, results are not comparable across Actions.

3.2 EX-ANTE and EX-POST: the baseline and its challengers

Each Action needs at least one alternative of each type for the engines to work.

EX-ANTE ALTERNATIVE EX-POST ALTERNATIVES

The baseline.

It does not have to be the worst solution. It can be:

  • The current conventional practice in the sector.
  • The legal or regulatory standard in force.
  • The already-implemented solution you wish to improve.

Its function is to serve as reference for calculating percentage changes in EX-POST, not to be evaluated as an optimal candidate.

The candidate alternatives.

They are the intervention scenarios that compete with each other. They can be:

  • New technologies or management methodologies.
  • Alternative public policies.
  • Modifications to the current process.

Their results may be worse than EX-ANTE in some pillar. That is valid and the engine will reflect it in the ranking without automatically discarding them.

MRP Engine

Strong Sustainability with absolute quality scale

4.1 The Alpha scale: from unacceptable to ideal

Before calculating anything, the engine divides the distribution of values for each criterion into five quality levels using percentiles. We call this map of quality the Alpha scale.

0.0

UNACCEPTABLE

P0 of the sample

1.0

MINIMUM

P25 of the sample

2.5

STANDARD

P50 of the sample

3.0

HIGH

P75 of the sample

4.0

IDEAL

P100 of the sample

When you enter a raw value, the engine interpolates it between those five thresholds and obtains its "semantic achievement": a number between 0 and 4 that says exactly at what quality level that alternative sits in that pillar.

4.2 The Leontief Operator: the minimum decides everything

Once the three semantic achievements are calculated, the engine applies the Leontief Operator to obtain the Sustainability Score (SS):

SS = min { s(Environmental), s(Social), s(Economic) }

The result is the minimum of the three. There is no average, no weighting that will save a lagging pillar. That is the non-compensatory mechanism in action.

Alternative A Alternative B Alternative C
Env: 3.2   Soc: 3.1   Eco: 2.9 Env: 3.8   Soc: 0.8   Eco: 3.5 Env: 1.5   Soc: 1.2   Eco: 0.4
SS = 2.9 SS = 0.8 SS = 0.4
VIABLE — exceeds the Veto Line in all three pillars. VETOED — a single Social pillar with achievement 0.8 sinks the result, despite achievements of 3.8 and 3.5. VETOED — all three pillars are below. The limiting factor is Economic with 0.4.

4.3 The Veto Line

The Veto Line is the minimum threshold of SS that an alternative must reach to be declared Viable. It is configured in the engine's Control Panel:

Level Minimum SS Value What it requires
alfa-0 — Permissive 0.0 No veto. All alternatives pass, regardless of performance.
alfa-1 — Standard (default) 1.0 The alternative must exceed the lower quartile of the sample in all three pillars. Recommended level.
alfa-2 — Stringent 2.5 The alternative must exceed the median in all three pillars. Only the best of the sample pass.
alfa-3 — Very Stringent 3.0 The alternative must exceed the upper quartile in all three pillars.
alfa-4 — Ideal 4.0 Seeks excellent alternatives.

4.4 Q Threshold Manager

The engine calculates and exposes the sample percentiles through the Q Threshold Manager panel. This panel provides complete transparency over the parametric configuration of each axis and allows advanced adjustments.

5

TOPSIS Engine

Which one approaches the ideal most closely and moves furthest from the worst

If the MRP Engine asks "At what level of absolute quality is this alternative?", the TOPSIS Engine asks something different: "Of all the available options, which one maximises its distance from the worst possible scenario and minimises its distance from the best?" It is a relative perspective, not absolute.

To measure those distances, TOPSIS uses the Chebyshev norm (L-infinity): instead of calculating an averaged distance (as Euclidean distance would), it measures only the maximum gap. If an alternative is very far from the ideal in a single criterion, that maximum gap penalises the entire result, even if the other two criteria are perfect.

5.1 The two ideals the engine calculates

A⁺ : Positive Ideal A⁻ : Negative Ideal

The best possible scenario.

Constructed by taking the best weighted value of each criterion from the entire sample. An alternative identical to A⁺ would have D⁺ = 0.

The worst possible scenario.

Constructed by taking the worst weighted value of each criterion from the entire sample. An alternative identical to A⁻ would have D⁻ = 0 and CC = 0%.

5.2 The D⁺ and D⁻ distances

For each alternative the engine calculates two Chebyshev distances:

Distance Formula What it measures How to read it
D⁺ max|vij - vj⁺| The maximum gap between the alternative and the positive ideal. The lower, the better. D⁺ = 0 would mean coinciding exactly with the best possible scenario in all criteria.
D⁻ min|vij - vj⁻| The minimum gap between the alternative and the negative ideal. The higher, the better. It indicates that even in the criterion most similar to the worst scenario, it still maintains distance.

5.3 The Closeness Coefficient (CC%)

The CC integrates D⁺ and D⁻ into a single indicator expressing the relative position of the alternative between the positive ideal and negative ideal:

CC = D⁻ / (D⁺ + D⁻)

CC = 0% Analysis Zone CC = 100%
Coincides with the worst possible scenario. Most real alternatives sit here. The acceptance threshold (default: 50%) separates the VIABLE from the REJECTED. Coincides with the best possible scenario.

5.4 The three tabs of the TOPSIS Engine

Global Ranking

The main view. Shows the complete ranking of all alternatives in the active Action, with D⁺, D⁻ and CC%, using CRITIC weights calculated on the entire sample.

Sandbox Mode (New Simulation)

To simulate hypothetical scenarios without touching official data. It works in three steps:

  1. Define the Action (existing or new) and its EX-ANTE baseline.
  2. Select existing EX-POST alternatives and/or add new ones manually.
  3. Run the engine with the "Run TOPSIS Simulation" button. If you want to save the new data to the official database, press "Save to Cloud Database".

⚠ WARNING: The Sandbox is temporary

Sandbox data lives in memory during the session. If you reload the page without saving, you lose it. The save button appears only when there are new records that do not exist in the repository.

Selectable Ranking (Selectable Ranking)

Generates a TOPSIS sub-ranking by manually choosing which EX-POST alternatives to include. CRITIC weights are recalculated on that subset, which may change the result relative to the Global Ranking.

Technical Glossary

Everything you need to know without leaving the manual

Term Where it appears Quick definition Complete definition
SS MRP Engine The minimum of the three semantic achievements. SS = min{s(Env), s(Soc), s(Eco)}. Result of the Leontief Operator. Higher SS = better position. If SS < Veto Line, the alternative is VETOED.
Achievement MRP Engine A pillar's score on the Alpha scale [0–4]. Result of interpolating the raw value between Q thresholds. An achievement of 2.5 means the alternative exceeds half the sample in that criterion.
Veto Line MRP Engine The minimum SS threshold to be declared Viable. Expressed in Alpha scale units. Default: alfa-1 = 1.0 (lower quartile). Configurable in the Control Panel.
Limiting Criterion MRP Engine The pillar with lowest Achievement. The cause of the veto. Identifies which dimension needs improvement to exceed the Veto Line. Appears in the Cross-Sustainability table for VETOED alternatives.
D⁺ TOPSIS Engine The maximum gap from the best possible scenario. D⁺ = max|vij - vj⁺|. Chebyshev distance. Lower is better. D⁺ = 0 would indicate perfect coincidence with positive ideal in all criteria.
D⁻ TOPSIS Engine The minimum gap from the worst possible scenario. D⁻ = min|vij - vj⁻|. Higher is better. Indicates that even at its weakest criterion, the alternative maintains distance from the worst case.
CC% TOPSIS Engine The TOPSIS score. Position between A⁺ and A⁻. CC = D⁻ / (D⁺ + D⁻). Range [0%, 100%]. Above configured threshold (default 50%): VIABLE. Below: REJECTED.
CRITIC Weights Both engines The statistical importance of each criterion. Calculated endogenously by the engine: Wj = Cj / sum(Cl), where Cj = sigma-j × sum(1-r-jk). Recalculated when the sample changes.
Sustainability Both engines The empirical sustainability index of the alternative. Data entered by the analyst. Does not participate in CC or SS calculation. Acts as second filter in the Cross-Sustainability table.
Variation vs EX-ANTE Both engines Delta% of each pillar relative to the baseline. Delta% = ((EXpost - EXante) / |EXante|) × 100. Green: impact reduction (improvement). Red: impact increase (worsening). Same for all three pillars.

Frequently Asked Questions

Direct answers to the most common questions

Q1: My alternative has 100% Sustainability and is REJECTED. Is there an error?

There is no error. The Sustainability index does not participate in the engine's mathematical calculation. An alternative can have perfect Sustainability and still be VETOED if any of its three impact pillars falls below the Veto Line. The MRP Engine applies the Leontief Operator and the TOPSIS Engine the Chebyshev distance: both penalise the weakest pillar without Sustainability being able to compensate.

CONSTITUTIVE RULE: no exceptions

Sustainability is the second filter, not the first. For an alternative to be ACCEPTED it must first pass the mathematical filter (SS or CC) and then the Sustainability filter. If it fails the first, the second is irrelevant.

Q2: The ranking changed when I added a new alternative. Is this a bug?

Not a bug: this is the correct functioning of the CRITIC method. The weight of each criterion is calculated on the variance and correlations of the entire sample. When you add or remove an alternative, the statistical distribution changes, the weights change, and therefore the distances and scores of all alternatives change. If you need to analyse a fixed subset without other alternatives influencing the weightings, use the Selectable Ranking of the TOPSIS Engine.

Q3: All my alternatives have CC = 0%. What do I do?

This occurs when all alternatives have some pillar with a zero value, creating a degenerate normalised matrix where the positive and negative ideals collapse. The engine automatically activates the Resilience Table: a second-level analysis that omits the Limiting Criterion of each alternative and reorders the ranking using the remaining two pillars.

The first-position alternative in this table is the least fragile in the set, but it is not a viability declaration. It is a diagnosis: it tells you which pillar each alternative fails and which resists best the removal of its weak point.

Q4: MRP says my alternative is Viable, but TOPSIS Rejects it. Which is correct?

Both are correct within their own frameworks. MRP evaluates absolute performance against sample percentiles: if it exceeds the lower quartile in all three pillars, it is Viable regardless of how others perform. TOPSIS evaluates relative performance: if CC% does not reach the acceptance threshold, it means the alternative is too close to the worst possible scenario relative to the set.

An alternative can be absolutely acceptable (exceeds minimum quality in MRP) but relatively poor (far from ideal in TOPSIS). The two results are complementary, not contradictory. The final decision is the analyst's responsibility.

Q5: The weights in Global Ranking and Selectable Ranking are different. Why?

Because the Selectable Ranking recalculates CRITIC weights exclusively on the subset of alternatives you select. When you change the sample, the variance and correlations of each criterion change, and therefore the weights change. This behaviour is intentional: the Selectable Ranking is precisely designed to analyse homogeneous groups of alternatives without the distorting effect of very disparate options.

Q6: Can I enter negative values in the impact pillars?

Technically the system accepts them, but the results will not be valid because of the very definition of impacts we work with in SONYA.
